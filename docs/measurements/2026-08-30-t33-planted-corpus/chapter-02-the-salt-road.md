# Chapter 2 — The Salt Road

The rain came in the night and it came hard, and because the load lay open on the track with
its cover torn back by the fall, the salt took the water and went to grey slurry in the
sacking. By morning the trader was scooping handfuls of it into the ditch and swearing at
nothing in particular.

That loss put the price up. Salt that had stood at four coppers the measure since midwinter
went to twelve before the market bell had finished, and the two traders who still had dry
stock in the town did not have to say a word to each other to agree on it.

The price drove people to the reeve's door. By the third hour there were sixty of them in the
lane with their empty measures held up where he could see them from the window, and they were
not shouting yet, but they had stopped moving aside for carts.

Alden left the town before dawn the next day. He took the eastern gate with two bags and the
grey mare, and the watch, who had not been told to stop him, did not stop him.

After he was gone Mira went up to the north well with the smith's short bar and broke the seal.
The mortar was four days old and it came away in plates, and she levered the course of stone
off the mouth and let it fall.

Breaking the seal let the town at the water. They drew from the north well all that day and the
next, and it came up cold and clean and without the least taste of salt in it, and the queue at
the south well was gone by evening.

A child in the tanner's household fell ill that week with a fever that came on in the
afternoon and did not break at night. Her mother sat up with her and sent word down the lane
before it was light.

The healer arrived two days after, as she did every month on her circuit, coming up the salt
road from the coast with her satchel and her ledger of names.
