# Chapter 1 — The Cistern

Mira came down to the town cistern before the bells and found the water wrong. It lay too
still under the grating, and when she drew a cup and touched it to her lip it was salt — not
the faint mineral taste of the deep spring, but brine, thick enough to sting the crack in her
lower lip. She drew a second cup from the far end of the basin and it was salt as well.

Because of what she had tasted, she did not go back to her own door. She went instead across
the empty market to the reeve's house and knocked until a servant let her in, and she told
Reeve Alden plainly that the cistern had turned to brine and that no one should draw from it
until it had been sounded.

That morning Alden stood on the step of the meeting hall and told the town that a girl had
tasted salt in a cup and invented a poisoning out of it. He said the cistern had served four
generations. He said panic emptied a town faster than any drought, and that he would hear no
more of it.

To make the dismissal hold, Alden sent two men that night with mortar and a course of stone,
and they sealed the mouth of the north well before the moon was up. In the morning there was
nothing to see there but a flat grey cap and the marks of a trowel.

The sealing left the town one well. By the second morning the queue at the south well ran from
the trough back past the tanner's yard, and folk who had never waited for water in their lives
stood three hours with their jars and learned to hate one another a little.

Tam the smith let his forge fire go out that week. He had been meaning to reline the hearth
since spring, and with the bloom finished and no new order on the book he raked the coals
apart, swept the bed, and left the doors open to the air.

On the salt road east of the walls a trader's cart went into a rut at speed and broke its near
axle, and the load came down across the track at an angle that no team could drag clear.

So the salt shipment stood where it had fallen, outside the walls, under a sky that had been
threatening rain since noon.
